![King Bibi](<../assets/images/S2CP7JIMJBJHZPUVVYU7AE5SQU (1).jpg>)
As the Israeli elections took place on Tuesday, many people and politicians were on the edge of their seat waiting for the results. According to the Associated Press, at first everybody was angry because they had not chosen a winner, but Netanyahu's Asslik party edged out with 51 out of the 120 seats of Israel's "government". Israeli elections do not appoint a dictator directly, but instead the people vote to give parties seats in the government, and their representatives vote for the prime minister. In the rare event of the Asslik party not holding the majority to win (60 votes), parties must form a coalition to actually decide things. The Arab-majority parties won a record 19-seats, and they have been nicknamed "socialists" by Donald Trump. When the prime minister is decided, The Shallot will report.
